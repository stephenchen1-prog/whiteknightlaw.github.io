Fraud help

What to do after fraud, a scam or identity theft

If you have lost money to a scam or someone is using your identity, these steps help you limit the damage and build the record that banks, police and agencies will ask for. You do not have to do them all today. Start at the top.

  1. Write down what happened while it is fresh. Note dates, amounts, account numbers involved, names, phone numbers, websites and how you were contacted. Save screenshots, emails and texts. Do not delete anything, even if it is embarrassing.
  2. Call your bank or card company's fraud line. Ask them to stop or reverse payments where possible, close or freeze affected accounts, and send you their fraud claim in writing.
  3. Report identity theft at IdentityTheft.gov. This is the Federal Trade Commission's site. It gives you an official Identity Theft Report and a recovery plan.
  4. Report scams and fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If the fraud happened online, also report it to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov.
  5. Freeze your credit. A freeze is free and stops new accounts being opened in your name. Contact each of the three bureaus: Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. You can check your credit reports for free at AnnualCreditReport.com.
  6. File a police report. Your local police department can take a report even if the scammer is far away. Ask for the report number and keep it with your records.
  7. Ask about victim services. In New York, the Office of Victim Services funds local victim assistance programs and may help with some costs. Other states have similar offices.

Specific situations

Where else to turn

  • Older adults: the U.S. Department of Justice's National Elder Fraud Hotline is 1-833-372-8311.
  • Investment fraud: you can submit a tip or complaint to the Securities and Exchange Commission at sec.gov/tcr.
  • Free legal help in New York: LawHelpNY.org lists free legal services by county and topic.
  • Danger or an emergency: call 911.

Questions

Common questions

Is this my fault?

No. Scams are run by people who do this for a living, and they work on smart, careful people every day. Reporting helps you and helps stop it happening to someone else.

Can White Knight Law be my lawyer?

No. We are not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. We can help you get organized and point you to legal aid and victim services.

Does this cost anything?

No. The guidance on this page is free, and so is reporting to the agencies listed here. Be wary of anyone who asks for a fee to recover money you lost. Recovery scams are common.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Agencies and their websites can change, so check the agency's own site before you act.
